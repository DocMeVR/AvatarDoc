## When to use this

A face toggle sets a few shapes, such as a smile. Gestures, face tracking or other toggles can still move the other shapes of the face at the same time, and the pose looks different each time. A rule that holds the rest keeps every other shape of that mesh still while the entry is on.

A rule works on a toggle, and on a trigger that has effects of its own. A radial cannot have one, because it has two ends instead of on and off. A trigger in <Label k="ad.ui.touch.flip" /> mode cannot have one either.

To hold one shape at one value in every animation, all the time, see the guide about overrides.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. In the Hierarchy, select the object that holds your AvatarDoc component.

2. In the tree, select the face toggle, or make one: press <Label k="ad.ui.add.toggle" /> on the toolbar and name it, such as Smile.

3. In the toggle's panel, press <Label k="ad.ui.add_effect" />, then <Label k="ad.ui.blendshapes" />, and pick the shape the pose sets, such as Smile.

   You should now see a <Label k="ad.ui.blendshapes" /> section with a row for the shape at weight 100. If the toggle already has blend shape rows, skip this step.

4. In the <Label k="ad.ui.blendshapes" /> section, press <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold" /> and pick the face mesh.

   You should now see a rule under the rows. Its switch reads **Everything else on Face**, with the name of your mesh. <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_at" /> is set to <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.value.default" />, and a note says how many shapes the rule can hold.

5. In <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_at" />, choose what the other shapes are held at:
   - <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.value.default" /> keeps each of them at the weight it has in the scene when you upload.
   - <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.value.zero" /> clears them all to 0.

6. Leave <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_blink" /> and <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_visemes" /> off, so blinking and lip sync keep working. Turn one on only when the pose must hold the eyes or the mouth too.

   <Shot name="guides/hold-the-rest-rule" alt="A Smile toggle's blend shapes with 1 Hold the rest, 2 the rule's switch, 3 Hold at, 4 Hold blink and 5 the note with the count." />

   In the picture, 1 is <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold" />, 2 the rule's switch, 3 <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_at" />, 4 <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_blink" /> with <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_visemes" /> beside it, and 5 the note.

</Steps>

<Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_blink" /> shows only for a mesh the avatar blinks with, and <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_visemes" /> only for a mesh it speaks with. Turned on, <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_blink" /> holds the blink shapes too and pauses VRChat's blinking while the entry is on. <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_visemes" /> does the same for the mouth shapes and lip sync. Both come back as soon as the entry is off.

### Capture a pose from the scene

If you set the face up in the scene first, <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.capture" /> makes the rows for you.

<Steps>

1. In the face mesh's renderer component, set the blend shape weights to the pose you want.

2. In the toggle's <Label k="ad.ui.blendshapes" /> section, press <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.capture" /> and pick the face mesh. The section shows once the toggle has a blend shape row: add one first, as in step 3 of the first part.

   You should now see a row for every shape that is higher than 0 in the scene, at its scene weight and set to <Label k="ad.ui.seg.pin" />. On a toggle, Capture also adds a rule that holds every other shape of the mesh at 0, or switches on the rule the mesh already has, which keeps its <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_at" />.

</Steps>

On a trigger, <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.capture" /> adds the rows alone. Use <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold" /> to add the rule.

### What the rule holds

The shapes the entry has rows for keep the values of their rows. A rule never holds:

- the shapes a toggle's own custom animations move
- the shape a blend shape trigger follows
- the shapes an AvatarDoc Shapes component drives, so a piece of clothing keeps changing the body
- the blink and mouth shapes, unless their switch is on

A toggle holds the rest while it is on, and leaves those shapes alone while it is off. A trigger holds them while it fires.

The note says "up to" because AvatarDoc writes only what is needed at upload. It holds the shapes that something else on the avatar moves: another toggle, a gesture, face tracking or another tool's animation. With <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.value.zero" />, it also holds the shapes that are not at 0 already. Every other shape never moves, so it already shows the value the rule would hold it at. A shape that only something outside the avatar moves, such as a dance in an MMD world, is not held.

A rule adds no parameter and costs no synced bits.

## Check it worked

- Press <Label k="ad.ui.summary" /> in the inspector's header. The summary lists each rule with its mesh, how many shapes it can hold, and what it holds them at.
- In Play mode with Gesture Manager, turn the toggle on, then make a hand gesture that changes the face. The face keeps the toggle's pose. See [Your first toggle](../../start-here/first-toggle/#test-it) for Play mode.

## If it does not work

- Blinking or lip sync stops while the toggle is on: <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_blink" /> or <Label k="ad.ui.shape_set.hold_visemes" /> is on. Turn it off.
- One shape still moves while the toggle is on: the toggle has a row for that shape, an AvatarDoc Shapes component drives it, or only something outside the avatar moves it.
- An error says only a toggle or a trigger with effects of its own can hold the rest: the rule sits on a radial or on a trigger in <Label k="ad.ui.touch.flip" /> mode. See [the message](../../reference/messages/#ad-error-shape-set-not-a-toggle).
