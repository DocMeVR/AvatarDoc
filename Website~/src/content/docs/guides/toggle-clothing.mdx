## When to use this

Use a toggle when one press should show or hide something: a hat, a jacket, or a jacket and its scarf together. The same toggle can also hide one piece while it shows another.

If only one of several outfits may be worn at a time, see [Make one-of-many choices](../one-of-many/). To build a whole outfit menu in one window, see [Your first outfit selector](../../start-here/first-outfit-selector/).

## Steps

<Steps>

1. In the Hierarchy, select the object that holds your AvatarDoc component.

2. In the inspector's toolbar, press <Label k="ad.ui.add.toggle" />.

   You should now see a toggle named New Toggle, selected in the tree. Its panel has an empty <Label k="ad.ui.objects" /> section.

3. In the toggle's panel, type a name into <Label k="ad.ui.name" />, such as Jacket, and press Enter.

4. In the Hierarchy, drag the jacket onto the <Label k="ad.ui.objects" /> section of the panel.

   You should now see a row with the jacket, set to <Label k="ad.ui.seg.shown" />.

5. To change more pieces with the same press, drag each of them onto the section too. Each piece gets its own row.

6. For a piece that should disappear while the toggle is on, press <Label k="ad.ui.seg.hidden" /> on its row.

   <Shot name="guides/toggle-clothing-objects" alt="A Jacket toggle's panel with 1 its Objects rows, 2 the Shown and Hidden choice of a row, and 3 the Scene preview buttons." />

   In the picture, 1 is the <Label k="ad.ui.objects" /> section, where the jacket is shown and the hoodie is hidden while the toggle is on. 2 is the choice on each row. 3 is the <Label k="ad.ui.preview" /> row you use in the next part.

7. To start with the toggle on when the avatar loads, tick <Label k="ad.ui.default_on" />.

</Steps>

You can also press <Label k="ad.ui.add" /> in the <Label k="ad.ui.objects" /> section and then the small circle at the right end of the new row. A list of your avatar's objects opens, with a search field.

To make many toggles at once, drag objects from the Hierarchy onto the menu tree instead. Each object becomes its own toggle, named after the object.

While the toggle is off, AvatarDoc hides every <Label k="ad.ui.seg.shown" /> piece and shows every <Label k="ad.ui.seg.hidden" /> piece. It does this whatever the piece looks like in the scene, so you do not have to switch pieces on or off in the Hierarchy before you upload.

## Check it worked

- In the toggle's panel, press <Label k="ad.ui.preview.on" /> and then <Label k="ad.ui.preview.off" /> in the <Label k="ad.ui.preview" /> row (<Label k="ad.ui.mode.advanced" /> mode). The Scene view shows the pieces change. Press <Label k="ad.ui.preview.default" /> to stop the preview. The preview needs NDMF's previews: turn them on under **Tools > NDM Framework > Enable Previews**.
- In Play mode with Gesture Manager, press the toggle in the menu, as in [Your first toggle](../../start-here/first-toggle/#test-it).

## If it does not work

- The Scene view does not change when you press <Label k="ad.ui.preview.on" />: NDMF's previews are off, or the AvatarDoc preview is off under **Tools > NDM Framework > Configure Previews**. The inspector's header says so while they are off.
- The piece stays visible in the game: another toggle or animation on the avatar also switches that piece. Check the other toggles in the tree for a row with the same piece.
- The toggle is not where you expect it in the menu: <Label k="ad.ui.in_menu" /> is off in its panel, or its submenu is full. A submenu holds eight controls, and AvatarDoc moves the extra ones to a More page.
